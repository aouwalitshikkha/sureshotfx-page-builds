SMC basics

What are smart money concepts?

Smart Money Concepts (SMC) is a way of reading price that focuses on where liquidity sits and how institutions move through it. The book uses six ideas.

Smart Money Concepts (SMC)

A framework that reads market structure, liquidity and institutional order flow instead of relying on lagging indicators.

Liquidity sweep

A move through a known high or low that triggers resting stop orders, then reverses. Also called a stop hunt or raid.

Market structure break (MSB)

A decisive break of a swing high or low that changes the short-term trend. A break is not the same as a sweep.

Order block

The last opposing candle before an impulsive move. Traders watch it as a potential reaction zone when price returns.

Fair value gap (FVG)

A three-candle imbalance where price leaves a gap in delivery. Markets often return to rebalance it before continuing.

Breaker zone

A failed order block that flips into an opposing zone. Often the sharpest reversal area when structure agrees.

Where does liquidity sit?

Liquidity pools are clusters of stop orders. The book maps six types. These are the four most visible on a normal chart:

Higher-time-frame swing highs and lows

The first pools institutions target.

Equal highs and equal lows

Obvious stop clusters that invite a raid.

Asia session range

Sets the liquidity table for London and New York.

Previous day and week levels

Reliable external reference points on any chart.

Execution

How does the SMC entry model work?

The model waits for three things to agree: market structure, a liquidity raid and a point of interest. If one is missing, there is no trade.

  1. 01

    Confirm structure. Identify the trend and the last structure point.

  2. 02

    Wait for the raid. Let price sweep the liquidity pool, then look for a reaction.

  3. 03

    Mark the POI. Use the order block, FVG or breaker zone that price returns to.

  4. 04

    Take the trigger. Entry, stop and target are set before the trade, at a minimum of 1:2.

The basics

What Is Lot Size in Forex? Why Does It Matter?

Lot size is the trade volume you open — the number of currency units behind a position. In forex it comes in four standard sizes, each suited to a different account balance and experience level. The size you choose decides what one pip is worth, and therefore what a losing trade costs you.

Two traders can take the same EURUSD setup with the same stop and get completely different outcomes, because one risks 0.10 lots and the other risks 1.00. Getting the lot size wrong is the fastest way to overtrade an account.

Rule of thumb

Risk 1–2% of your balance per trade. On a $1,000 account that is $10–$20 — not $100.

What is a lot size calculator?

A lot size calculator — also called a forex position size calculator or a forex risk calculator — works backwards from your risk. You tell it your capital, the percentage you are willing to lose and the distance to your stop loss. It returns the trade volume that makes that stop cost exactly the amount you planned.

No manual pip maths, no guessing, no spreadsheet. The forex lot size calculator on this page runs the same formula every time, so two trades with the same risk profile get the same risk in money terms — even when one is EURUSD and the other is gold.

What is a position size calculator?

The same thing under a different name. Position sizing is the risk-management term; lot size is the forex unit. A position size calculator answers "how big should this trade be?", and a lot calculator answers "how many lots is that?".

Lot size = Risk amount ÷ (Stop loss × Value per pip or point)

Risk amount = Account balance × Risk %

Using the Lot Size Calculator With MT4 and MT5

The calculator is platform-agnostic: a lot size calculator MT4 accepts is the same lot size MT5 accepts, because both use the broker's contract specification. What changes is where you type it.

In both terminals, open a new order, put the result in the Volume field (called "lots" in MT4), set the stop loss, and check the margin line before you click buy or sell. If the volume box refuses the number, your broker's minimum or step is different — try 0.01 and read the specification.

MT4 does not include a position size calculator, and neither does MT5 — which is why this page exists. Searching for an MT4 lot size calculator or an MT5 lot size calculator returns the same number; the platform only changes where you type the volume.

What Lot Size Should You Use?

There is no universal "best" lot size — only a size that fits your balance, your stop and the percentage you accept losing. Here is the rule most consistent traders use.

1% risk · 20-pip stop · EURUSD ($10 per pip per lot)
Balance Risk (1%) Lot size Per pip
$100$1Below 0.01—
$500$50.02$0.20
$1,000$100.05$0.50

Read the table as a starting point, not a target. A wider stop means a smaller lot for the same risk; a tighter stop means a larger one. The money at risk stays constant.

Leverage does not change your lot size

Leverage changes the margin required, not the loss. A 1.00 lot on a 1:500 account loses the same $10 per pip as on 1:30 — the difference is how much free margin the broker locks up.

Cent accounts

Cent accounts often use 1,000-unit lots and show the balance in cents. Enter the dollar-equivalent balance, use the result, then confirm the contract size in the specification — it may differ from a standard account.

Prop and funded accounts

Use the daily and total drawdown limits as your hard ceiling. Most funded traders size at 0.5–1% per trade so a losing streak cannot breach the rule in a week.

Lot Size Calculator — Common Questions

Everything traders ask about lot size, position sizing and the numbers behind the calculator.

What is a lot size calculator?

+

A lot size calculator works out the trade volume that keeps your loss at a chosen amount. You enter your balance, the percentage you want to risk and the distance to your stop loss; it returns the lot size. It is the same tool as a position size calculator or a forex risk calculator.

How to calculate lot size in forex?

+

Use the formula: lot size = risk amount ÷ (stop loss in pips × pip value per lot). On a $10,000 account risking 1% ($100) with a 20-pip stop and a $10 pip value, that is $100 ÷ (20 × $10) = 0.50 lots. The calculator above does the conversion when your account currency is not USD.

What is a position size calculator?

+

A position size calculator tells you how large a trade to open based on your balance, risk percentage and stop loss. It performs the same job as a lot size calculator — one names the risk concept, the other names the forex unit.

Can I use the SureShotFX lot calculator for a micro account?

+

Yes. The calculator returns standard lots, and 0.01 is a micro lot. If the result is below 0.01, the calculator warns you: the trade is too large for your balance and stop distance at that risk level.

Do I need to know the pip value manually?

+

No. The calculator derives the pip value from the pair and converts it into your account currency. You only need the balance, risk percentage and stop distance.

Does the calculator work for MT4 and MT5?

+

Yes. The lot size is platform-independent; you enter it in the Volume field of the MT4 or MT5 order ticket. Both terminals use the broker's contract specification, so the result matches as long as the contract size is the standard one. Check the specification for gold and indices.

How do I calculate lot size for gold (XAUUSD)?

+

Use the gold tab: one standard lot is 100 ounces, so one point (0.01) is $1.00 per lot and a $1.00 move is $100. Risk $50 with a 500-point ($5.00) stop and you get 0.10 lots. If your broker uses a different contract size, enter its value per point.

How do I calculate lot size for indices?

+

Find the value per point per lot in your broker's contract specification, then divide your risk by (stop in points × value per point). A $100 risk with a 100-point US30 stop at $1 per point is 1.00 lot. If your broker quotes $10 per point, the same trade is 0.10 lots.

What is the best lot size for a $1,000 account?

+

At 1% risk ($10) with a 20-pip stop on EURUSD, 0.05 lots. At 2% risk the same stop allows 0.10 lots. "Best" means the size that keeps your loss at the percentage you chose — not the largest size the margin allows.

How much is 0.01 lot size in dollars?

+

0.01 lots is 1,000 units. On a USD-quoted pair such as EURUSD it is $0.10 per pip, so a 20-pip stop risks $2.00. On gold, 0.01 lots is 1 ounce, so one point is $0.01 and a 500-point stop risks $5.00.

Does leverage affect my lot size?

+

No. Leverage changes how much margin your broker holds, not what a pip is worth. A 1.00 lot loses $10 per pip at 1:30 and at 1:500. Higher leverage lets you open a larger position — which is exactly why it is dangerous, not why it changes the correct size.

What lot size should I use on a cent account?

+

Enter the dollar-equivalent balance and use the calculator's result, then check the contract size in the specification. Cent accounts frequently use 1,000-unit lots, so the volume number can differ from a standard account even though the money at risk is the same.

Can I use this for prop firm or funded accounts?

+

Yes. Enter the funded account size and the risk your firm allows — many traders use 0.5–1% per trade. Keep the daily loss limit in mind: several small losing trades at 1% can still breach a 3% daily rule.

Why does my broker give a different lot size?

+

Three usual reasons: a different contract size (common on gold and indices), a different pip definition (0.0001 vs 0.00001 on five-digit quotes), or a different account currency conversion. Check the specification first, then the quote currency.
